Chapter 6: "GUILT"

Val approached the ends of the city, where Echo would be thrown off. Though the amount was nothing compared to how busy heaven usually was, there were still a noticeable number of angels headed to the same event.

All these people seemed to hate Echo. Some were chanting for him to be thrown off immediately, others held up signs. The guy was really not in the best of situations.

Val noticed this. He was supposed to be hated by everyone instead of Echo.

He knew that. Still, he couldn't determine whether what he had done was right or wrong.

Maybe he was taking this long to find an answer because he was afraid that the correct one would not be in his favor. Maybe the influence of violence was still pressuring him, even though he had gained some clarity in his mind. He was torn.

Finally, he and the people around him reached the far edge of heaven, and guards began to appear. Many of them were gathered in the area.

Echo was brought out from the main temple, about a twelve-minute walk from where the crowd was waiting.

Echo had an expressionless face. It almost looked like he didn't care.

From his perspective, there was nothing he could do except go along with what everyone wanted for him: to be thrown off to Earth, deemed unnecessary and unworthy of standing beside the others.

They carried him by the arms, and he did not resist.

As they approached the end of the path, more and more people gathered around Echo and the guards, fueled by hate and dismay.

Many shouted at him. Many glared at him with fierce looks. Some even threw things at him.

The atmosphere was unsettling, to say the least.

Val saw Echo pass by and looked him in the eye.

Echo returned the eye contact. His face flashed with realization.

Val rushed to the end of the path, curious about what would become of Echo.

Through the crowd, he could see Echo being brought to the edge, where a cliff stood—commonly known as the gate to Earth.

Then, Echo was mercilessly thrown off.

The crowd cheered.

Val's chest tightened.

Was it him who should have been in Echo's place?

His heartbeat sped up, and the crowd only cheered louder.

The lingering guilt in the back of Val's mind grew heavier, and he looked down for a moment.

Breathing became difficult.

He hurried away from the scene, unable to endure it any longer.

Val and Caelira walked toward Val's home, where they would eat.

The streets were empty and quiet.

Heaven was shaken.

Caelira's voice broke the silence between them.

Caelira: [Oh! I forgot to ask—how are you doing? With the whole violence thing? How'd you react to it?]

Val kept his eyes on the ground.

Val: [I'm—I'm fine.]

The word came out too quickly.

Was that suspicious?

Caelira started humming softly, a song Val had heard before but couldn't recognize.

They walked on.

Caelira: [Poor Ace. You think he deserved it?]

Val almost stopped moving.

For a fraction of a second, his body resisted moving forward. Then he forced himself to keep walking.

It was a question he had already asked himself countless times.

He had spent the last nine days circling it.

He opened his mouth to answer.

Nothing came out.

He tried again, clearing his throat first.

Val: [Well—I don't kno—I-I mean, we can't… we can't know. Ahem.]

His breathing slipped out of rhythm.

His voice carried a thin, anxious tone he couldn't hide.

For now, it was the truest answer he could give.

Caelira glanced at her son, then looked ahead.

Caelira: [You know that kid they ended up throwing down? Echo, I think his name was. Wasn't he one of your closest friends?]

Val didn't answer.

His gaze stayed fixed on the ground, like it was the only thing he could hold onto.

Val: [...Oh. Yeah. You could say that.]

They finally reached the house.

Before entering, Caelira spoke once more.

Caelira: [Well, I don't really think he was the one who killed Ace.]

Before Val could ask for an explanation, Caelira hurried inside.

Val's heart raced.

That statement could mean many things.

One of them was that she knew what Val had done.

His mind leapt to that conclusion instantly, ignoring other possibilities.

Had his mother really uncovered the truth?

They sat at the table, already prepared by the servants.

The room felt uneasy.

Food was served—lavish, appetizing dishes.

To Val, it looked like vomit on a plate.

Caelira spoke first, swiftly changing the topic.

Caelira: [When I was at the main temple the other day—the big one—they explained some things I found interesting.]

Val stared at his plate.

He hadn't picked up his fork.

He was listening, confused and disjointed.

That possibility—that she knew—still lingered.

He raised his head with a small gasp.

Val: [Y-Yes.]

Caelira remained calm.

Caelira: [Since you're my son, I'll let you in on the fun.]

Val's gaze returned to his plate.

Caelira: [You know how that old granny's head exploded? At that gem shop near where Ace was killed.]

Val's hand twitched.

His fingers nearly refused to close around the fork.

What had happened was undeniably his fault.

Caelira: [A theory says many survived due to three factors: intelligence, soul strength, and how the concept of violence was presented to them.]

Val raised his head again, relief briefly washing over him.

This was another topic.

Caelira: [You seem shaken for someone who went through one of those courses. How come?]

The relief vanished.

Was this concern—or something else?

Val tensed.

His chest felt too tight.

Val: [W-Well, everyone reacts d-differently, I… I guess.]

His voice trembled.

He forced himself to eat.

It was harder than it should have been.

Caelira: [You okay?]

Her voice was gentle.

To Val, it felt accusatory.

Caelira: [I suppose you're right. You'll get used to it.]

False alarm.

His shoulders loosened slightly.

Val: [I-I'm fine. You shouldn't worry.]

Caelira nodded sympathetically.

Caelira: [You know, I think what the guards did was wrong.]

She paused.

Caelira: [I don't think Echo killed Ace.]

Val raised his head, fork frozen mid-air.

Caelira: [Call it intuition. An educated guess.]

His heart skipped.

Caelira: [Maybe it's because I know his family.]

To Caelira, it was a quiet conversation.

To Val, the walls closed in.

Val: [I'm sorry—]

Only he heard it.

The space around him felt too small.

Val: [Well—who w-was it then? Who do y-you think… it was?]

Caelira answered calmly.

Caelira: [We can't know, I suppose.]

She leaned closer.

Val's breathing spiked.

Caelira: [But I do know one thing.]

She paused.

Val stared, frozen.

Caelira: [Whoever did it should throw themself off to Earth too.]

The fork and knife slipped from Val's hand.

They clattered against the plate and fell to the floor.

Val: [I'm so sorry. I'm sorry. I—I'm sorry. Really. So sorry…]

The words spilled out, drowned by the sound of metal.

Not for the cutlery.

But for the realization that had just struck him.

Val's throat closed.
